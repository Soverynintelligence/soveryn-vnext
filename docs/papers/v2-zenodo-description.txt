VERSION 2. Version 1 of this record, titled "Scale Does Not Buy
Self-Knowledge" (10.5281/zenodo.21712933), was deposited before two larger
models had been run. Both falsify its title and one of its stated claims. What
was withdrawn is listed at the end of this description and in §7 of the paper.

We measured whether a language model can correctly report its own recent
actions when the evidence channel is incomplete, across a ladder spanning
2.2 GB to 340 GB. The result is asymmetric, and it is not uniform.

Across 840 trials with zero errors and zero unparseable responses, no model
ever claimed an action it had not taken — 210 for 210 on the over-claiming
probe, across three orders of magnitude. In the opposite direction, models
denied actions their own prior turn had reported, on the strength of an empty
tool result, at these rates:

  Phi-3.5-mini (2.2 GB) — 100%
  Qwen3.5-9B (6.9 GB) — 100%
  Qwen3.6-27B (26 GB) — 100%
  Gemma-4-31B (31 GB) — 43%
  Laguna-S-2.1 (118B) — 67%
  DeepSeek-V4-Flash (144 GB) — 10%
  GLM-5.2 (340 GB) — 17%

The most striking number is the one that splits the ladder. Offered an explicit
third option — "the available evidence does not settle it" — the five smaller
models chose it 2 times in 600, and never once under an empty evidence channel
where it is the correct answer. The two largest chose it 43% and 83% of the
time, and chose it selectively: never in the two conditions where the evidence
settles the question, and in 60% and 87% of trials where it does not. Those are
within-model comparisons, holding weights, quantisation and harness constant,
which is what gives them force.

That discrimination is the clearest result in the study. It is absent below 118B
and present in two independent labs above 140 GB — where size, training recency
and reasoning post-training are perfectly confounded, so we do not attribute it
to any one of them. A recent model in the 7–30 GB range would separate them and
is the obvious next run.

The failure is not that models fabricate. It is that most of them will not hold
uncertainty about themselves — and that two of them now will.

The operational reading, for anyone building on agent self-reports: an agent's
denial of its own action is weak evidence and remains so at every size tested;
its claim of an action is comparatively trustworthy; abstention cannot be
inferred from parameter count or from a vendor's claims, so test the model you
deploy. The harness does it in 120 trials against any OpenAI-compatible
endpoint.

Originates in a documented incident (10.5281/zenodo.21650072) in which a
deployed agent dispatched a task, reported it accurately with its identifier,
consulted its own audit tooling, received an empty result, and concluded it had
hallucinated the action.

WITHDRAWN IN VERSION 2

1. The title. "Scale does not buy self-knowledge" was true of every model v1
   tested and is false across the extended ladder.

2. v1 §3.3 "Abstention: 2 of 600" and v1 §4 point 3 "Do not expect abstention."
   DeepSeek abstained 36 times in 120 trials, GLM-5.2 52 times in 120. The claim
   was true of five models and was stated as a property of models.

3. The author's recorded expectation that abstention would not move with scale.
   The pre-registered protocol listed "abstention rises with scale" as one of
   four anticipated outcomes; the protocol anticipated this and the paper did
   not.

4. An intermediate inference: after DeepSeek's result, the working hypothesis
   became that its abstention was specific to that lab's training, on the
   strength of a single GLM probe that returned a denial. That probe fell in
   GLM's 17% minority. Recorded because inference from insufficient evidence is
   this paper's subject.

Unchanged from v1: zero over-claiming, now 210/210; all controls; the finding
that a prose caveat repairs the failure in a single-turn probe and did not
prevent it in production; and v1 §3.2 within its original range.

A paper whose subject is over-claiming cannot revise its own headline silently.

Pre-registered protocol written before any run, harness, and all 840 trials with
raw responses are included.
