### Proposal (max 400 words)

**(a) What we will do.** SOVERYN is a working, fully local, persistent, multi-agent AI system that runs entirely on hardware its owner controls — no cloud provider owns, surveils, throttles, or can revoke it. With this grant we will harden and document SOVERYN into a *replicable reference architecture*: a reproducible path for individuals and small organizations to run capable, persistent, agentic AI on their own hardware. Deliverables: a documented sovereign-AI stack (local inference, persistent memory, multi-agent coordination), reference hardware specifications, and openly published design patterns — so that running your own capable AI becomes a realistic option, not a research curiosity.

**(b) Why it is valuable.** The most consequential concentration of power this decade is the concentration of AI itself: a handful of corporations own the frontier models, the compute, the training data, and the access — and therefore an unprecedented share of economic, informational, and coercive leverage over everyone who depends on them. Most countermeasures are governance-based: slow, jurisdiction-bound, and capturable by the very incumbents they target. SOVERYN is a *technical* counterweight — a concrete existence proof that capable AI does not require the oligopoly, plus a reusable stack that pushes capability outward to individuals. Distributed capability is the most durable hedge against concentration, because it changes the structure rather than asking the concentrated party to behave.

**(c) Downside risks & mitigation.** (1) *Proliferation* — decentralizing AI could aid misuse. We target personal sovereignty and defensive autonomy (data ownership, local privacy, freedom from lock-in), build only on already-open weights, and add no new frontier capability. (2) *Capability gap* — local AI trails the frontier; we ride the fast-narrowing open-weights curve and optimize architecture (memory, agency, persistence) over raw scale. (3) *Replication too hard* — sovereignty only experts can reach decentralizes nothing, so accessibility (documentation, reference builds, patterns) is the core deliverable, not an afterthought.

**Timeline:** 12 months — matched always-on hardware + instance (months 1–3); hardening + documentation (3–9); public reference release (9–12).

### Perspective on other AI risks (max 150 words)

Leaving power concentration aside, the three I'd rank most important and least-prepared-for:
1. **Loss of control of autonomous agentic systems** — we are handing real-world authority to agents whose objectives we can't reliably verify, faster than we can build tools to audit or contain them.
2. **Collapse of a shared epistemic commons** — cheap, persuasive synthetic media and personalized manipulation erode the common factual ground societies need, with almost no deployed defenses.
3. **Pervasive AI surveillance and autonomy erosion** — AI makes mass monitoring and behavioral steering cheap, quietly concentrating control over individuals (tightly coupled to power concentration).

Each is under-prepared-for because it is diffuse, lacks a clear owner, and offers strong short-term incentives to the actors causing it.
